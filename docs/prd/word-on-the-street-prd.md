# Word on the Street — PRD

## Summary
Word on the Street is a newsroom-style pulse system that surfaces what’s moving right now and provides verification trails. The initial focus is AI tooling and engineering decision support, with a broader vision that spans product intelligence, local pulse, and culture trends.

## Problem
- Algorithms show more of what you already like, not what’s objectively moving.
- Search only answers what you already know to ask.
- Engineers and curious people need signal, not a feed loop.

## Goals
- Provide a high-signal pulse for AI tooling and engineering decisions.
- Preserve an anti-algorithmic, verification-friendly experience.
- Deliver briefings that are easy to share with teams.

## Non-Goals
- Real-time social feed replacement.
- Personalized recommendation engine.
- Deep investigative fact-checking (we provide trails, not adjudication).

## Primary Users
- Software engineers in AI who need to track tooling, frameworks, and platform shifts.

## Secondary Users
- Curious general audiences who want the pulse beyond their bubble.
- People researching products or local events.

## Value Proposition
A newsroom for the internet: send out reporters, get back the pulse, and follow verification trails to check sources.

## Product Requirements
- Multi-source signal collection across major platforms.
- Clear reporting that surfaces what’s moving and why.
- Verification trails to support fast fact-checking.
- Shareable briefings that work as standalone artifacts.

## Success Metrics
- Repeat usage for the same beat (weekly or daily).
- Briefing share rate within teams.
- User-reported trust in signal quality and verification trails.

## Risks
- “Pulse” dilutes into another feed if personalization is overused.
- Report delivery feels noisy or redundant without a clear editorial lens.

## Future Ideas / Roadmap
### Now (0–3 months)
- AI tooling pulse with daily/weekly briefings.
- Decision support for tool selection (signals + verification trails).
- Shareable briefing artifact v1.

### Next (3–6 months)
- Product intelligence reports (issues, firmware problems, sentiment).
- Single-file briefing bundle (HTML + CSS + JS) shareable like a PDF.
- Email delivery with interactive sections.

### Later (6–12 months)
- Local pulse (city/neighborhood briefings).
- Culture and trend radar (fashion, music, micro-trends).
- Audience-specific briefing modes (teen/parent formats).

## Open Questions
- Should “product intelligence” be a named sub-brand or a feature mode?
- What verification trails are essential for consumer trust?
- How much personalization is acceptable without breaking the anti-algorithmic ethos?
